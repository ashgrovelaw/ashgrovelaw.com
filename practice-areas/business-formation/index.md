# Forming the right entity

For most small businesses in Ohio the real choice is between a limited liability company and a corporation, and then how that entity is taxed. An LLC taxed as an S corporation can save an owner-operator several thousand dollars a year in self-employment tax once the business is profitable, but it adds payroll and bookkeeping costs. A C corporation makes sense if you plan to raise venture capital. We walk through the numbers with you and your accountant and recommend one.

## What a formation includes

Our standard formation package is billed at a flat fee and includes:

- filing Articles of Organization or Incorporation with the Ohio Secretary of State;
- a statutory agent, so your company has a registered address for legal notices;
- an EIN application with the IRS;
- a written operating agreement or bylaws drafted for your business, not a fill-in-the-blank form;
- organizational resolutions and an ownership ledger;
- a one-page checklist of the state and local registrations your business needs, such as Ohio withholding, the Commercial Activity Tax and the City of Columbus income tax.

## Agreements between owners

If you are going into business with someone else, the operating agreement matters more than the filing. A good one answers the questions partners would rather not ask out loud: What happens if one of us wants out? If one of us dies or divorces? If we disagree and it is fifty-fifty? How is a departing owner's share valued, and how quickly does the company have to pay for it?

We draft buy-sell provisions, vesting for founders who are still earning their share, and deadlock procedures, and we review agreements you already have when the business has outgrown them.

## Keeping the company healthy

After formation we act as outside general counsel for many of our business clients: annual updates to ownership records, admitting new members, raising money from friends and family, and, eventually, selling or winding down the business.
